# SRAM AXS Battery Life: How Long It Lasts & Care Tips

A SRAM AXS derailleur battery is good for roughly 20 hours of ride time per charge, and the coin-cell battery inside each shifter typically lasts about two years before it needs swapping. Both batteries give you a warning before they die outright, but only if you know where to look. Here's how the charge cycle actually works and how to avoid getting stuck in one gear mid-ride.

![Close-up of a SRAM AXS electronic derailleur and battery mounted on a bicycle drivetrain](/images/articles/sram-axs-battery-life.webp)

## How Long an AXS Battery Actually Lasts

Each AXS derailleur — front or rear — runs on its own removable battery, and SRAM rates that battery for about 20 hours of active ride time per full charge. That number holds up well in normal conditions, though how you ride affects it: a lot of shifting under load (steep, technical terrain) draws more power per shift than spinning along on flatter roads, so two riders logging the same mileage can see somewhat different battery drain.

The shifters run on a separate, much smaller battery: a standard coin cell (CR2032) tucked inside each shift paddle. Because the shifter only wakes the battery briefly to send a wireless signal, that coin cell lasts far longer than a derailleur charge — [check your maintenance schedule](/articles/bike-maintenance-schedule) and plan on replacing it roughly every two years, sooner if you notice the shifter responding sluggishly.

## Reading the Charge Indicators

Every AXS battery has a small LED on the housing that tells you where you stand before you clip in:

- **Solid green** (or a quick green flash when you press the button): plenty of charge.
- **Flashing red**: getting low — charge soon, don't start a long ride on it.
- **Solid red / no light**: the battery is dead or nearly there.

Shifters have their own low-battery signal too — most AXS systems flash the shifter's LED or throw a warning in the SRAM AXS app when a coin cell is running low. Get in the habit of a quick glance at both derailleur LEDs before you head out; it takes five seconds and it's the single easiest way to avoid a mid-ride surprise.

## Cold Weather Behavior

Lithium-ion batteries — which is what AXS derailleur batteries are — lose usable capacity in cold temperatures, the same way an EV or a phone battery reads lower in winter. A battery that shows two bars at 70°F can drop to one bar once the temperature falls into the 30s or 40s, even though the actual charge hasn't changed much. The capacity comes back once the battery warms up, so don't panic and toss a battery that seems to have died on a cold ride — let it sit at room temperature and check it again before writing it off.

Practically, this means charging a little more often in winter and not trusting a borderline charge indicator on a cold morning the way you would in summer.

## The Spare-Battery Strategy

AXS derailleur batteries are interchangeable between the front and rear derailleur, and that's the whole point of carrying a spare: if one derailleur battery dies mid-ride, you can pull the battery off the other derailleur and get the rear shifting working again (front shifting matters far less if you're just trying to get home). Keep a charged spare in your jersey pocket or saddle bag on long rides, group rides, or anything remote enough that a dead battery means walking.

A charger and a spare battery cost far less than the time lost bailing out of a ride early — it's cheap insurance for anyone doing back-to-back long days.

## Storage Mode for Off-Season Bikes

If a bike is going into storage for a season — winter, an off-bike injury, whatever — pull the AXS batteries off the derailleurs rather than leaving them mounted and draining. Lithium batteries self-discharge slowly even when idle, and a battery left sitting at zero for months can lose capacity permanently. Store batteries at a partial charge (not full, not empty) in a cool, dry spot, and give them a top-off charge before the bike comes back out. For the rest of the bike — chain, tires, sealant, and where to keep it — see the full [winter bike storage guide](/articles/winter-bike-storage).

## Getting Stranded in One Gear

When an AXS derailleur battery dies completely, the derailleur doesn't fail open — it holds its last position. That means you're stuck in whatever gear you were in when the battery quit, which is manageable on flat ground and rough on a climb. This is the scenario the spare-battery strategy above is built to prevent, and it's also why a quick LED check before every ride matters more with electronic shifting than it ever did with a cable.

## Monitoring Instead of Guessing

Checking two tiny LEDs before every ride is easy to forget, especially once electronic shifting starts to feel routine. Building AXS battery checks into the same habit as [checking tire pressure and brakes before a ride](/articles/pre-ride-bike-check) is the low-effort fix — treat it as one more line on the same pre-ride pass rather than a separate thing to remember.
