# Gravel Bike Tire Pressure: How to Find Your PSI

There is no single right PSI for a gravel bike. Your best pressure depends on your weight, the bike and gear you carry, tire width, whether you run tubeless, and the surface you ride. Start from a calculator, then adjust by feel until the bike grips, rolls, and protects the rim.

![Cyclist checking tire pressure with a gauge on a gravel road](/images/articles/gravel-bike-tire-pressure.webp)

## What Moves Your Number

- **Total weight:** rider, bike, and anything you carry. More weight needs more pressure.
- **Tire width:** wider tires run lower pressure for the same support.
- **Tubeless or tubes:** tubeless setups can run lower without the pinch flats that tubes suffer from.
- **Surface:** loose gravel, hardpack, and washboard each reward a slightly different setting.

## Start From a Calculator

Most major tire and wheel brands publish pressure calculators. Enter your weight, tire width, and setup, and use the result as your first number. Treat it as a starting point, not a rule, since each calculator makes its own assumptions.

## Adjust by Feel

Change pressure in small steps and ride the same section each time.

1. If the bike skips and chatters over washboard, you may be too high.
2. If the tire squirms in corners or the rim bottoms out on rocks, you may be too low.
3. Settle on the lowest pressure that still feels stable and protects the rim.

Many riders run the front slightly lower than the rear. Test it on your own routes.

## Too Low Has a Cost

Very low pressure raises the risk of pinch flats and rim damage. If you run tubeless, low pressure and old sealant make a bad pair, so check your [sealant schedule](/articles/tubeless-sealant-how-often) too.

## Check Pressure Before You Ride

Tires lose air between rides, and pressure also shifts with temperature and altitude. Add a pressure check to your [pre-ride bike check](/articles/pre-ride-bike-check) so the number you worked out is the number you ride. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should front and rear pressure match?

Not necessarily. Many riders run a little less in the front for grip, but the right split depends on your weight distribution and terrain.

### Does temperature change my pressure?

Yes. Air pressure moves with temperature and altitude, so recheck before rides after big changes in weather or elevation.

### Can I run lower pressure with tubeless?

Generally yes, because there is no tube to pinch. You still need to protect the rim, so lower in small steps.

### How often should I check pressure?

Before every ride. It is one of the fastest checks in your routine.
